Evidence #001 Premieres Wed Oct 28

Utilization

The balance that gets reported. Your score never sees what you owe today — it sees the balance standing on the day your statement closes. Keep the reported balance under 30% of the limit; the healthiest bodies report under 10%.

Mort: “Your limit is not a target. It's a fence.”

Evidence #002 Filed

The Statement Date

The balance that gets charged on. Interest feeds on the average daily balance — not the number on your due date. Pay the day the statement closes and the same money starves it: carry $2,000 all month and it feeds on $2,000; pay on day one and it feeds on half.

Mort: “The due date is their deadline. The statement date is yours.”

Evidence #003 Filed

The Minimum-Payment Trap

The payment that gets accepted. On a $5,000 balance at 21% APR, paying only the minimum takes 19 years and $7,708 in interest — more than the debt itself. Freeze the payment at a fixed $200 a month and the same body is buried in 34 months, for $1,633.

Mort: “The minimum is designed to shrink. So the debt never has to.”
