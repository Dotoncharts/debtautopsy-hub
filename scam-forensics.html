Scam Forensics

Some debts you sign up for. Others arrive by text message. Scam Forensics is the Toxicology lab inside Debt Autopsy: the scams, junk fees and fine print that poison wallets — opened up on the same table, with the same receipts standard as every case file.

The Lab Rule Mort only autopsies the already-charged. Every named case in this lab traces to a regulator action or a court record — allegations are labelled allegations, and nothing goes on the table on a rumour. If it isn't charged or documented, it isn't dissected.
Toxicology #001 Tue Nov 3

The Delivery Text

A text lands: your parcel is held, and a $1.99 fee frees it. The two dollars were never the theft — they were the key being cut. In the specimen on Mort's table, that key opened the door to $480 in three charges. The episode closes with the red flags: save them.

Mort: “Two dollars is never the theft. It's the key they're cutting.”

Scam Forensics #002 Tue Nov 10

The Debt-Relief Fee Harvest

The robocall promised to settle his cards for half what he owed. What it harvested was $1,211 in fees — while he was told to stop paying his creditors. The balance grew $2,000, the score fell 150 points, and the number went dead. He was never the customer.

Mort: “He wasn't the customer. He was the harvest.”

Scam Forensics #003 Tue Nov 17

The Doubling

Send one, get two back — the oldest trick in a new costume. He sent $500 to the address on the livestream. Nothing came back; the address went dark. Every case in this lab ends the same way: with the red flags, in plain sight, where they can't be unseen.

Mort: “If he could double money, he wouldn't need yours.”
